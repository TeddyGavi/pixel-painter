# Bead palettes

One TSV per brand series is the source of truth. `gen_palettes.py` turns each into
`main/palette_<id>.c/.h`; the generated files are committed but never edited by hand.

```sh
python3 palettes/gen_palettes.py      # after editing any palettes/*.tsv
```

TSV columns: `code r g b name source note`.
- `code`: the manufacturer's colour code. It is what the order report lists.
- `source`: `label` (RGB printed by the manufacturer) or `photo` (sampled from a chart
  photo, approximate). The firmware's `bead_color_t.approx` is 1 for `photo`.
- `name`: descriptive and **unofficial** where the brand publishes no names
  (generated by `colorname.py`, then free to edit by hand).

Order in the TSV = order of chips on the device's palette strip.

## Method (all brands)

The device's export is a bead order list, so **codes must match the manufacturer's
chart exactly**. Colours only need to look right on screen. Every brand follows the
same pipeline; the sections below record what differed.

1. **Locate the tiles** on the official chart (image or PDF page render), from the grid
   geometry or from the label positions.
2. **Read the codes exactly.** Use the PDF text layer when there is one (Hama). Otherwise
   OCR the labels and check every shipped code by eye against its crop (Artkal,
   Miyuki). Check the count against the brand's stated total, and that codes are unique.
3. **Get the colour.** Use the RGB printed on the label when the chart has one
   (`source = label`). Otherwise take the median of the bead pixels in the swatch photo,
   white-balanced, and mark it `source = photo` (firmware `approx = 1`).
4. **Check in CIE Lab:** label vs photo dE for labelled colours, `uniformity` (the share
   of bead pixels within dE 15 of the median) for photo colours, and pairwise dE for
   near-duplicates.
5. **Name** each colour with `colorname.py` where the brand publishes no names.
6. **Write the TSV** (scripts in `tools/`, see each brand), then run `gen_palettes.py`.

A palette holds at most **254 colours**: a cell stores a `uint8_t` palette index and
`0xFF` means empty. A larger range has to be curated down (Miyuki).

## Artkal S (`artkal_s.tsv`): 225 colours, 5 mm midi, hard

- **Source:** Artkal's official S-series colour chart image ("S-5MM color chart, 225"),
  `https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1323/8195/files/artkal-beads-S-series-color-chart-_2.webp?v=1757662815`,
  linked from `https://www.artkalfusebeads.com/blogs/artkal-color-chart` (retrieved
  2026-11-10). Artkal publishes the chart only as an image: no text, CSV or PDF.
- **Codes and RGB (176 solid colours, S and SE):** the RGB printed on each swatch's label.
  Tiles were located on the chart grid, and each label was enlarged 4x and read by eye
  (Tesseract could not read the ~6 px digits reliably). Checks:
  - all 225 tiles found, matching Artkal's stated count;
  - all codes unique, all values 0-255;
  - every label was compared with the colour sampled from its own swatch photo. Hue
    disagreements were inspected individually. Only **S119** disagrees beyond photo
    lighting: its label says green (113,196,82) while the photo is pale mint. The
    printed label is kept, and this is noted in the TSV.
  - **SE06**'s label is identical to S24's (20,123,209); kept as printed.
- **Specialty beads (49, `source = photo`):** translucent ST1-8, glitter SL1-6,
  glow-in-the-dark SG1-7, neon SN1-4, pearl SP1-8, UV-changing SUV1-4 and two-colour
  striped SD1-12 carry no RGB on the chart. Their colour is the median of the swatch
  photo's bead pixels, so it is only an on-screen approximation. Names carry the type
  as a prefix (Clear, Glitter, Glow, Neon, Pearl, UV, Stripe).
- **Names:** the S chart prints no names (Artkal's slip has them; they are not on the
  web), so names are generated from the RGB: lightness plus hue family, with
  neutrals, browns, beiges and olives named separately. Several colours share a name;
  the code always disambiguates.
- `tools/extract_tiles.py` and `tools/sheets.py` are the scripts used to locate tiles and
  build the enlarged label sheets for reading.

Not included yet: Artkal's newer J midi range (S + J = 443 colours), and the other
series (R soft midi, C/A/M mini).

## Miyuki Delica (`miyuki_delica.tsv`): 254 of ~1,270 colours, 11/0 cylinder

- **Source:** Miyuki's Delica Beads colour chart, cards 1-7,
  `https://www.miyuki-beads.co.jp/common/data/pdf/N-2.pdf` (retrieved 2026-11-13). The
  PDF is seven 300 dpi photos of the printed cards: codes and bead strips only. It has
  no text layer, no RGB and no names.
- **Why 254:** the palette cap (see Method). The chart has about 1,270 codes (DB, DBC
  cut, Duracoat, Picasso and so on).
- **Extraction:** a page-wide `tesseract --psm 11` pass found about 40-60% of the code
  labels. That was enough to fit each card's columns (with photo tilt) and its row
  pitch. Every cell was then cropped. Its label was binarised (gridlines removed) and
  read with `--psm 7`, and the bead strip beside it was sampled.
- **Colour (`source = photo`, all `approx = 1`):** the median of the strip's central
  bead band, white-balanced so the paper beside the strip reads 245. It is an on-screen
  approximation; the code is what matters for ordering.
- **Selection:** greedy farthest-point sampling in CIE Lab, seeded with DB-10 (black)
  and DB-200 (white). It is weighted toward uniform-looking beads (high
  `uniformity`, see below) over AB, metallic and mixed finishes. Every chart bead is
  within dE 16 of a shipped colour.
- **Codes are exact:** OCR confuses 5/6/7/8 on this font, so all 254 shipped codes were
  checked by eye against the label crop from the chart. All matched.
- **`sources/miyuki_delica_all.tsv`:** every cell found, with colour, `uniformity` (the
  share of bead pixels within dE 15 of the median) and card number. Only `verified=1`
  rows (the shipped set) have checked codes. The other codes are raw OCR, so check one
  on the chart before moving it into `miyuki_delica.tsv`. `gen_palettes.py` does not
  read `sources/`.
- **Names:** generated by `colorname.py`, as for Artkal. They are unofficial; Miyuki's
  names, such as "Opaque Luster Rose", are not in the PDF.

Perler (a 6-colour starter) was removed when Miyuki was added. Saved patterns store a
brand index, so `store.c` (pattern v4) remaps v2/v3 saves: Hama 1→0, Artkal 2→1. Old
Perler saves fall back to defaults.

Miyuki tools, in run order (scratch dir `S` holds `work/`): `tools/miyuki_cells.py`
(per page: grid, OCR, crops), `tools/miyuki_merge.py` (merge, flag bad OCR, duplicates
and out-of-sequence codes), `tools/miyuki_curate.py S 254` (colour, uniformity,
selection), `tools/miyuki_sheets.py` (verification contact sheets) and
`tools/miyuki_write_tsv.py S palettes`.

## Hama midi (`hama.tsv`): 88 colours, 5 mm

- **Source:** Hama's colour chart PDF ("Hama 99198 Farvekort 2026"),
  `https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0726/3771/0492/files/colour-chart-number.pdf?v=1777215620`
  (retrieved 2026-11-13). One A4 page: an 11 x 8 grid of 88 bead photos, each with its
  code printed below. It has no RGB and no names.
- **88, not 122:** codes run 01-122, but 34 numbers are not on the chart (23, 25, 33,
  39-42, 50-54, 57-59, 65-69, 80, 81, 85-94, 99, 100). The highest code is 122; the
  chart has 88 colours.
- **Codes are exact:** the PDF has a text layer, so codes come straight from
  `pdftotext -bbox` (no OCR). They are kept as printed: two digits with a leading zero
  (`01`), three digits from 100. The TSV is in chart order, row by row.
- **Tiles:** the page is rendered at 400 dpi. Each tile is located from its own code's
  position in the text layer: left from the label's x, top from the row's median y.
  The label's own x is used rather than the column median, because tile 03 is printed
  4.3 pt right of its column.
- **Colour (`source = photo`, all `approx = 1`):** the median of the photo's bead pixels
  after dropping the darkest 20% (hole shadows) and brightest 10% (highlights) by Lab
  L*. The photos contain no paper, so one global white balance uses bead **01 (White)**
  as the neutral reference, scaled to 245 grey (Miyuki's target). The gain is about
  1.12 / 1.09 / 1.11 (R / G / B). Thirteen bright colours clip at 255, mostly in red
  (02, 03, 04, 06, 32, 35, 38, 43, 72, 78, 79, 95, 121). The clipping merges no
  colours that the unclipped values kept apart.
- **Note column:** the chart marks each code with dots: blue = also made as **Mini**
  (2.5 mm), green = also made as **Bio**. They are read by counting blue and green
  pixels to the right of the code in the label band (starting 20 px below the label
  top, clear of the photo's bleed), and recorded as `also Mini, Bio`
  or `midi only`, with the tile's `uniformity`. 79 codes have Mini, 15 have Bio, and
  nine are midi only: 55, 56, 61-64 and 72-74. These nine look like specialty finishes
  (glow, metallic, pearl, glitter), but the chart does not say.
- **Names:** generated by `colorname.py`, as for Artkal. They are unofficial: Hama's
  names are not in this PDF. Specialty beads get plain hue names, so 61 (gold) is
  "Tan", 62 (silver) is "Dark Grey", 63 (bronze) is "Brown", 64 (pearl) is "Peach" and
  19 (clear) is "Light Grey".
- **Validation:**
  - the 88 codes in the TSV were checked against the text layer: same set, same
    row-major order, same formatting;
  - all codes are unique;
  - the Mini and Bio dots were checked by eye on the page for 03, 37, 55, 56, 61-64
    and 72-74, and on contact sheets for all 88. The review caught 55 flagged as Bio:
    its pale-green photo bled into the top rows of the label band. The band now starts
    below that bleed. Unflagged tiles count 0 dot pixels and real dots about 870, with
    the threshold at 600;
  - the review caught tile 03 sampled at its column position, which picked up a
    sliver of page background. Tiles are now placed at their own label position;
  - near-duplicates (dE < 5) are distinct products on the chart and are kept:
    45/74 (dE 1.3), 60/79 (2.5), 04/35 (4.4), 38/79 (4.5) and 18/71 (4.9).
- **Caveats:** colour is approximate. Low-uniformity tiles are mixed or
  specialty-looking photos, so their single colour is rough: 63 (0.38), 122 (0.39),
  102 (0.44), 07 (0.46), 10 (0.49), 117 and 119 (0.54), and 101 and 26 (0.55).
  Translucent beads (13-16, 19) are sampled as photographed.

Hama tools, in run order (scratch dir `S`): `pdftotext -bbox chart.pdf bbox.html`, then
render the page at 400 dpi (`pdftoppm -r 400 -png chart.pdf hi`), then `tools/hama_extract.py bbox.html hi-1.png
S/work_hama/hama.json S/work_hama/crops`, then `tools/hama_sheet.py S` (contact sheets
for checking) and `tools/hama_write_tsv.py S/work_hama/hama.json palettes`.

## Toho Round (`toho_round.tsv`): 207 colours, 11/0 and 8/0 seed beads

- **Source:** Toho's "Round Beads 11/0 8/0 Colors" sample card **No.111-1**,
  `https://www.tohobeads.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/111-1.pdf` (retrieved 2026-11-22).
  The PDF is one 441 ppi photo of the printed card (3649 x 5301 JPEG, Illustrator CS5,
  2014). It has no text layer, no RGB and no names. The card covers both sizes with one
  set of codes; the device lists the code only, so say 11/0 or 8/0 when ordering.
- **Codes are exact:** the card is a ruled table, five columns, each row a code cell and
  a bead-strip cell. Cells are found from the ruled lines (a thin-line filter, since the
  rules are only ~30 levels darker than the paper), not from OCR. All 207 cells were
  found; that matches a hand count of the card. Codes were OCR'd per cell and then
  **every one was checked by eye** on contact sheets. OCR was wrong on 13: 7BD, 47H, 25B,
  26B, 29B, 108BD, 116B and the six `S` codes 2151S-2156S. The corrections are listed in
  `tools/toho_write_tsv.py`. Codes are kept as printed (`2152S`, `23BDA`).
- **Order and sections:** TSV order is card order, column by column. The card's section
  headings are in the note column and give the name prefix:
  Transparent (46, `Clear`), Opaque (26), Ceylon (7), Transparent Silver Lined (65,
  `Silver-Lined`), Ceylon Silver Lined (28, `Ceylon SL`) and Transparent Luster (35,
  `Luster`). A table that starts a column without a heading continues the section above.
- **Colour (`source = photo`, all `approx = 1`):** every strip sits at the same place in
  its cell, so a fixed band through the middle of the strip is sampled (y0+37..56,
  m+85..280 in card pixels). Locating it per cell by "differs from paper" failed on
  white and pale beads, where only the drop shadow differs. The median is taken after
  dropping the darkest 20% and brightest 10% by L*, as for Hama. White balance is per
  cell: the paper in the **code** cell beside it is scaled to 245 (Miyuki's target). The
  strip cell's own paper is tinted by bead bleed, and the card is vignetted toward the
  bottom (gains 1.02-1.13).
- **Note column:** section; the care symbols beside the code, as words; and the strip's
  `uniformity`. Symbols, from the card's legend: `bleach` ● may change by bleach or
  oxidation; `friction` ★ may come off with friction; `friction+` ☆ with strong or
  prolonged friction; `solvent` ■ may come off with organic solvent (dry-cleaning,
  perfume); `solvent+` □ with prolonged exposure; `UV` ▲ may fade in sunlight; `light` ※
  looks different under different light (1300 only). ◆ (nickel-plated) is in the legend
  but on no bead on this card. Symbols were read by eye with the codes.
- **Names:** `colorname.py` with the section prefix, e.g. `Silver-Lined Amber`. They are
  unofficial: Toho's names are not on the card.
- **Validation:**
  - 207 cells, 207 unique codes, same sequence as the card;
  - contact sheets show the sampled band on every strip, to check its placement;
  - near-duplicates (dE < 4) are common because the same glass comes in several
    finishes, e.g. 42D/111B (dE 0.3), 10B/111 (0.7), 49/2205 (0.8). They are distinct
    products and are all kept;
  - 20 bright colours clip at 255 in one channel after white balance (5, 5B, 10, 10B,
    12, 42, 42B, 42D, 50, 50A, 25, 30, 30B, 2216, 2112, 102, 109, 111, 111B, 630).
- **Caveats:** colour is approximate. Silver-lined beads include their bright lining in
  the sample. Transparent beads are sampled against the white card. Low-uniformity
  strips (mixed photo) are 35 (0.35), 36 (0.38), 2156S (0.39) and 2216 (0.43).

Toho tools, in run order (scratch dir `S`): `pdfimages -j 111-1.pdf card` (the embedded
photo, not a re-render), then `tools/toho_cells.py card-000.jpg S/cells.json S/crops`,
then `tools/toho_color.py S/cells.json S/crops`, then `tools/toho_sheets.py S/cells.json
S/crops S/sheets` (contact sheets for checking codes, symbols and the sample band), then
`tools/toho_write_tsv.py S/cells.json palettes`. The corrections in `toho_write_tsv.py`
are by cell index, so re-check them if the cell finder changes.

## Preciosa Ornela, Czech beads (`preciosa_rocailles.tsv`): 254 of 667 colours, rocailles

- **Source:** Preciosa Ornela's "Seed Beads - Rocailles" sample cards (June 2013),
  `https://www.preciosa-ornela.com/content/files/downloads/Rocailles-Sample-Cards.pdf`
  (retrieved 2026-11-22). The colour pages are 2-6. Each row is a five-digit colour code
  and a photo of a bead strip, grouped into sections. Each section has an
  `ART No.` (e.g. `331 19 001`) and a finish title (e.g. "Transparent - Silver Lined").
  It has no RGB and no names. Sizes (13/0 to 4/0) share one set of codes.
- **Brand name:** the device shows **"Preciosa Ornela (Czech beads)"** in the Brand list
  and in exports (`palette_t.full`), since buyers know them as Czech beads. Places that
  cannot scroll (the main menu's Brand value, the "... locked" title, save slots) use the
  short `"Preciosa"` (`palette_t.brand`). The selected Brand row scrolls when it is too
  wide (see the change log).
- **Codes are exact:** they come from the PDF text layer (`pdftotext -bbox`), with no OCR.
  There are 667 rows and 613 distinct colour codes. The 54 repeats are all one pair: the
  shiny bead (ART 3x1 19 001) and the **matt** bead (ART 331 39 001) share a colour code.
  Matt beads therefore get the device code **`39-<code>`** (e.g. `39-10050`), using the
  ART group printed on the card. All other codes are exactly as printed. On page 3,
  `20206*` marks which of "Silver, Gold\*, Special Gold" is the gold. The asterisk is
  dropped from the code, and the note says "Gold".
- **Sections:** each row's section is its column's nearest ART header above it, and the
  title is the header's text lines. Page 6's "New arrivals 2010" block has one ART header
  (column 1) over four columns, each with its own title. A column with no header of its
  own uses the nearest header above in any column. All 667 rows have a titled section
  (59 sections).
- **Colour (`source = photo`, all `approx = 1`):** pages are rendered at 400 dpi. The
  strips are cut-outs on digital white, so bead pixels are everything that is not paper.
  The sample is the middle half of the strip's height, inset 5 px from its ends, after
  dropping the darkest 20% and brightest 10% by L* (as for Hama). There is no paper in
  the photos, so one global white balance uses bead **03050** (Natural Opaque, chalk
  white), mapped to 245 grey. The gain is about 1.10 / 1.03 / 1.04 (R / G / B), and 41
  bright rows clip at 255. Striped and two-tone beads come out as their average and
  score low on `uniformity`.
- **Why 254, and which:** the palette cap. The selection is Miyuki's method
  (`tools/preciosa_write_tsv.py`): greedy farthest-point in CIE Lab, weighted toward
  uniform strips, and seeded with black 23980 and white 03050. Every bead on the card
  is within dE 10.9 of a shipped colour. The median uniformity of the shipped set is
  0.85. 33 shipped codes are matt (`39-`).
- **Names:** `colorname.py` with a short finish prefix taken from the section title
  (`Clear`, `Opaque`, `Lined`, `Matt`, `Rainbow`, `Sfinx`, `Luster`, `Pearl`, `Metallic`,
  `Dyed`, `Stripe`, `Shell`, `Travertin`, `Cornelian`, `Glow`...). The first keyword
  found in `PREFIX` wins. They are unofficial. The note keeps the full ART No., section
  title and page.
- **`sources/preciosa_rocailles_all.tsv`:** all 667 rows with their device code, card
  code, ART, section, page, colour, uniformity and `shipped`. Codes there are exact
  too (text layer), so any row can be moved into the palette as is. It is not read by
  `gen_palettes.py`.

Preciosa tools, in run order (scratch dir `S`): `pdftotext -bbox chart.pdf S/bbox.html`,
`pdftoppm -r 400 -f 2 -l 6 -png chart.pdf S/hi`, then `tools/preciosa_extract.py
S/bbox.html S S/po.json S/crops`, then `tools/preciosa_sheets.py S/po.json S/crops
S/sheets` (contact sheets: row crop with the sampled band outlined, code, section,
colour), then `tools/preciosa_write_tsv.py S/po.json palettes`.
